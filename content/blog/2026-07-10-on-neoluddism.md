In 1791, the revolutionaries of France passed the _Allarde Decree_, part of
it reads (translated)

> It shall be free for any person to engage in such trade or profession as
> they see fit; however they must first obtain a patente tax, pay its cost
> as determined, and adhere to public order regulations.

The revolutionaries then, and as I think we should, saw the practice of
_guild protectionism_ as perhaps some of the last remaining vestiges of
feudalism. The _Allarde Decree_ does not, however, disawow the standards
set by the guilds - those would be absorbed by said public order 
regulations, thus dismantling the argument that only such a system can
provide the levels of safety created by said standards.

In 1868, after time and time again reform failed under the _bakufu_, the
ascendancy of the Meiji Emperor that finally banned the _kabu nakama_ (株仲間)
created a system in which reform could even happen. Emperor Meiji knew then,
as we do know, that monopoly under the guilds was not a path towards improving
the lives of the people.


On the other side of the
world, Karl Marx would warn in the Communist Manifesto (1848) that reinstating 
the system of guild protectionism is fundamentally **reactionary**. His vision
of communism will not ride on rolling back the progress that moved Europe from
a feudal mode of production to the capitalist mode of production.

---

I do not think that the punk band adding "FFO: neoluddism" to their posters
or the people creating the "luddite club" are fundamentally evil people who
actually want to limit the spread of ideas and the development of skills. I
do think, however, that they are playing right into the hands of people who
actually want to create a society that mirrors more closely to feudalism
than what we have currently.

Yes, I am claiming that neo-luddites are fundamentally no different from
neo-feudalists. 

You can certainly have scorn over the current state of affairs - it's bleak.
Every AI company keeps talking about how they'll take your job. Most job
postings on the internet are ghost jobs that you'll never hear from. Most
everyone my age is living paycheck-to-paycheck. I'm not saying this state
of things is defensible. I'm saying that the path to a more just world
does not lie in rolling back progress.

---

Back when I was a kid, I found it pretty disappointing the notion that you
had to get some amount of "years of training" to be a professional at something.
That you needed some sort of a certificate that you have been trained in something
to actually do it.

I was especially elated when I found out that this was not the case in real life.
That I could just play a guitar on the side of the street and someone would
eventually give me a few coins. Sure, you need at least a few months worth of
practice to get to that point but when I give a roadside pianist a coin it's not 
because of their ABRSM level. I'm not giving a coin to a roadside guitarist because
they graduated from Berklee. 

This is something deeply visceral for me. I cannot support the gatekeepers.
